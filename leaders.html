Why Local Leaders

Online Resources Aren't Enough

The expat experience is hyper-local. The best help comes from someone who lives in your city, knows the bureaucracy, and shows up in person. That's the role we're recruiting for.

🤝

Be the Trusted First Call

When a new American arrives in your city, you're the person they reach out to. You don't need to have every answer — you need to know who does.

🍽️

Host a Monthly Meetup

One casual gathering per month — coffee, dinner, drinks. We provide the templates, branded materials, and event listings. You provide the city.

📡

Bridge the Online–Offline Gap

Facebook groups and Reddit are noisy. Local leaders cut through with real, vetted, in-person support — and bring great signals back to AmericanAbroad.net.

🌟

Build Something Visible

Your city, your name, your photo on the AmericanAbroad leaders directory. Grow your local network and your reputation as the go-to American expat in your city.

The Role

What a Local Leader Actually Does

📋

Monthly Responsibilities

  1. Organize one local meetup per month

    Casual, low-key — coffee shop, restaurant, park. We provide event templates and a budget for venue/refreshments where useful.

  2. Be reachable to new arrivals in your city

    Listed on the AmericanAbroad leaders directory with your preferred contact (email, WhatsApp, Signal). Aim for a same-week response.

  3. Curate 1–2 local resource recommendations per month

    A great local tax advisor, a notaire who handles cross-border wills, an English-speaking pediatrician — share these with us so we can vet and add them to the Resource Hub.

  4. Join the monthly leaders call

    30 minutes, video, with all city leaders. Share what's working, what's not, and learn from leaders in other cities. Networking gold.

  5. Light reporting

    A short monthly form: how many attendees at your meetup, key topics that came up, any new signal worth knowing.

⏱️

Realistic time commitment

Plan on roughly 4–6 hours a month: planning + hosting one meetup, responding to local DMs, and the monthly leaders call. We're not asking for a part-time job — just a consistent monthly presence.

Profile

Who Makes a Great Local Leader

You don't need to be a tax expert, a lawyer, or a "professional expat." You need to be reliable, organized, and comfortable being the friendly first contact in your city.

🇺🇸

American citizen

You hold US citizenship (dual is fine). The community is built around the shared experience of being American while living elsewhere.

📍

1+ year in your city

You've already navigated the basics — paperwork, banking, finding a doctor — and can speak from experience.

🗣️

English + comfortable in the local language

Doesn't need to be fluent — but enough to help newcomers navigate everyday situations and recommend local resources.

📅

Reliable and organized

You'll commit to one meetup a month and a 30-minute leaders call. Showing up matters more than charisma.

💬

Genuinely helpful

You like helping people land softly. This isn't a sales role — it's a community-building role.

🌐

Comfortable being public-facing

Your name, photo, and city will appear on the leaders directory. Active social presence helps but isn't required.

FAQ

Common Questions

Is this a paid role?

It's a volunteer community role with optional revenue-share for partner referrals. Most leaders join for the visibility, network, and impact — not the income.

What if there's already a leader in my city?

We approve one primary leader per city to keep accountability clean. If your city is taken, we'll add you to the backup list — useful if the primary leader steps back, or if your city is large enough to add a second leader (e.g., a borough or arrondissement focus).

Do I need to be a tax or legal expert?

No. You're a community connector, not a professional advisor. When community members have specialist questions, you point them to the AmericanAbroad Resource Hub or a vetted partner. We give you the playbook.

Can I host events less often than monthly if my city is small?

Yes — for cities under ~2,000 estimated American residents we're flexible (every 6–8 weeks is acceptable). The monthly cadence is the target for medium and large cities.

Can I step back if my situation changes?

Absolutely. Just give us 30 days' notice so we can transition to your backup. No drama.
